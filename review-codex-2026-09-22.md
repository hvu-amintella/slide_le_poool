## A. What I retained

Amintella is presented as a France-based EdTech startup building AI tools for teachers to read and grade handwritten copies, compose exams, and eventually support interactive courses; it wants funding, technical/research support, and access to pilot schools.

## B. Scores

### Clear and easy to understand — 6/10

The deck only becomes clear on slides 3–4: the product, users, roadmap, and broad ambition are understandable, but slide 1 says nothing about the company and several important terms remain undefined. The product status, target establishments, pricing, pilot scope, and exact ask are not clear enough for a first-time audience.

### Not visually cluttered — 6/10

Most slides have a readable card structure and obvious hierarchy, but slide 2 is dense, slide 3 visibly contains unfinished placeholder content, and several annexes are either too empty or too small-text-heavy. Slide 5 has substantial unused space without enough explanatory content to justify it.

### Says something — 5/10

The retained message is “AI-assisted correction for handwritten schoolwork, expanding into a broader education platform.” The presented slides provide a roadmap and a funding request, but almost no proof, quantified ambition, customer evidence, or concrete commercial thesis.

## C. Per-slide notes

### Slide 1

**Clear**

Within ten seconds, I understand only that the company is called “AMINTELLA” and that this is a September 2026 presentation. The company category, problem, product, and audience are completely absent.

Suggested replacement subtitle:

> “L’IA qui corrige les copies manuscrites et aide chaque élève à progresser”

If that sentence is not exactly defensible, use a more neutral version:

> “Des outils d’IA pour les enseignants et les apprentissages”

**Not cluttered**

The slide is visually clean and easy to scan. It is arguably too empty for the first slide because the large visual space carries no thesis.

**Says something**

As a cover, it is functional. As the opening of an incubator pitch, it wastes the first ten seconds and forces the audience to wait until slide 3 to understand what Amintella does.

## Annexes

### Annexe A1 — Market

The slide communicates market scale, low AI adoption, and the existence of paper-based assessment. The figures need clearer sourcing and definitions: “14 %” and “36 %” need the exact survey question and population, while “~90 M h” is explicitly based on a calculation that is not shown. The right-hand panel is readable but the source line is too small for live use. Replace “~90 M h de correction par an dans le secondaire” with “≈90 millions d’heures de correction par an dans le secondaire”, and replace “Ensuite : les pays qui évaluent sur papier” with “À terme : les pays où les évaluations se font encore sur papier”. The slide still does not establish the reachable initial market or why these figures imply a viable business.

### Annexe A2 — Competition

The slide’s intended differentiation is understandable: handwritten reasoning, teacher validation, and learning from real copies. The quadrant looks more precise than the evidence shown: there is no scale, scoring method, or explanation for competitor placement, and the labels are small. Replace “L’enseignant valide dans l’outil” with “L’enseignant valide chaque note dans l’outil”, and replace “Les cours viennent des copies” with “Les futurs cours interactifs partiront des erreurs réelles de la classe”. The competitive claim remains unproven because no comparison, user evidence, or product demonstration is shown.

### Annexe A3 — Pilots

This is a clear list of four pilot metrics, but it contains no targets, baseline, sample size, duration, or decision rule. The slide is far too empty below the cards for what should be an important proof slide. Replace “Temps gagné” with “Minutes de correction économisées par copie” and “Satisfaction” with “Satisfaction de l’enseignant”. Without thresholds—for example, how much time saved or what validation rate is acceptable—the pilot plan does not yet define success.

### Annexe A4 — Student data

The slide states three important principles: human validation, regulatory awareness, and GDPR compliance. It is visually sparse, and the legal statements need a source, date, and more operational detail; “traitées dans le respect du RGPD” is a conclusion, not evidence. Replace “AI Act européen” with “Règlement européen sur l’IA (AI Act)” and replace the sentence with “L’évaluation des élèves relève des systèmes à haut risque au titre du règlement européen sur l’IA.” The slide still does not say where data is hosted, how long it is retained, who can access it, or what happens to student copies.

### Annexe A5 — Economic model

This is the most honest business-model slide because it distinguishes decisions from hypotheses. However, the right-hand card is dense, the left-hand card is under-filled, “Entrée” is unclear, and there is no price range, margin hypothesis, or expected pilot conversion. Replace “Entrée” with “Point d’entrée”, and add the missing space in “enseignants.Test” so it reads “enseignants. Test”. The slide says what must be tested but not what the company currently believes strongly enough to build around.

### Annexe A6 — Internal jury preparation

This is not an audience slide, and it should remain internal. It identifies serious unanswered questions: full-time commitment, company status, equity split, intellectual property, Rennes/Ille-et-Vilaine presence, pilot numbers, and twelve-month revenue. The problem is not presentation quality but preparedness: the deck currently knows these questions are likely and still leaves the answers blank. “Questions probables d’un jury d’incubation” could be replaced with “Questions probables du jury d’incubation”.

### Annexe A7 — Internal product and market preparation

This is also correctly marked as internal, but it exposes the weakest parts of the pitch: handwriting accuracy, the answer to “Pourquoi pas simplement ChatGPT ?”, competitive positioning, minor-data handling, teacher adoption, and AI cost per copy are all still qualitative. “Une boucle qu’un outil générique n’a pas” is too absolute without evidence; replace it with “Une boucle que les outils généralistes ne proposent pas dans ce contexte.” The missing figures for accuracy, cost per copy, pilot volume, and paid conversion remain material.

## D. Slide 6 plus script verdict

Slide 6 does stand on its own as a high-level closing ask, but not as a standalone explanation of the company’s needs. The script successfully supplies the missing detail about funding uses, GPU and research needs, target partner types, pilot timing, price validation, buyer validation, and what the Poool could provide.

Important information is still missing from both slide and script:

- The amount of capital sought and the runway it buys.
- The number and type of pilot establishments.
- The exact pilot success criteria.
- The founders’ transition to full-time work.
- The company’s legal status and capital structure.
- The intended geography of the first pilots.
- Any quantified commercial objective or twelve-month revenue target.
- The expected cost per copy and its effect on pricing.

The script is doing almost all of the explanatory work. The slide is acceptable for a live pitch, but it is too vague for anyone reviewing the deck without the founder present.

## E. Top five changes, ranked by impact

1. Add a one-sentence thesis to slide 1 stating the product, target user, and problem.

2. Repair slide 3 completely: remove “delete”, correct “customizé”, define “MVP”, and show the actual correction or exam-composition workflow.

3. Quantify slide 6: funding amount, runway, pilot count, pilot date, technical contribution sought, and the specific establishments or networks targeted.

4. Add proof: current users, signed or committed pilots, measured correction time, handwriting accuracy, validation rate, or another defensible result.

5. Make the roadmap and business model explicit: distinguish what exists today from what is planned, state who pays, and explain why the teacher-first adoption loop leads to parents and establishments.
